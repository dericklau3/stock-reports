## Thesis Assumptions That Must Remain True

1. Optical Communications remains a multi-year growth engine, not a one-year AI data center pull-forward.
2. Meta/NVIDIA/hyperscaler agreements convert into revenue and profit with acceptable margin, not just headline capacity commitments.
3. New U.S. optical connectivity and fiber capacity ramps without large cost overruns or quality problems.
4. Springboard 2030 upgrade comes with credible revenue, margin and ROIC targets.
5. Solar ramp costs are temporary; Q2 extended maintenance shutdown does not become a recurring profitability issue.
6. FCF improves as sales scale; capex and working capital do not absorb all incremental profit.
7. Debt and warrants remain manageable; potential NVIDIA warrant dilution does not materially impair per-share economics.

## Red-Line Conditions

Trigger a thesis review or downgrade if any occur:

- Q2 2026 core sales misses the guided ~$4.6B or core EPS misses $0.73-$0.77 without a credible explanation.
- Optical Communications growth slows sharply or segment margin deteriorates.
- Management discloses weaker-than-expected conversion from hyperscaler agreements.
- NVIDIA/Meta partnership details imply poor economics, heavy customer subsidies, or excessive Corning-funded capex.
- Solar costs continue to rise beyond the announced Q2 $30M incremental expense.
- TTM FCF fails to improve despite revenue growth.
- Net debt rises materially without visible return on capital.
- Customer concentration risk becomes explicit in filings or earnings Q&A.
